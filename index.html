Sangwoo Han

Postdoctoral Researcher, University of Washington, Seattle

sangwooh@uw.edu

I am a seismologist and a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Washington, working with Marine Denolle and Paul Bodin on multi-hazard, multi-sensor monitoring at Mount Rainier. I received my Ph.D. in Seismology and Geophysics from Seoul National University in 2026. My research uses dense seismic arrays and distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) to resolve fault structure and earthquake rupture complexity at sub-kilometer scales.

Research Highlights

Focal mechanisms of small earthquakes from 360° S-wave polarization

We showed that measuring the full 360° S-wave polarization at near-source stations recovers the double-couple radiation pattern, so that reliable focal mechanisms can be obtained from as few as two to three stations. Applied to a dense seismic array in Korea, the method determines focal mechanisms for events from M 2.9 down to M −0.4, and it is validated with the 2023 Parkfield and 2011 Mineral, Virginia, earthquake sequences.

Paper (GJI, 2026) · SSA Student Presentation Award

Multi-stage rupture of the 2021 Mw 4.9 offshore Jeju Island earthquake

Using local P and S waves and regional Lg waves, we resolved the complex rupture of this moderate earthquake on the southern Korea–East China Sea continental shelf. Relative source time functions show that the first subevent started with two weak nucleation phases and propagated slowly and bilaterally, and that a second subevent then ruptured westward. This cascading rupture of successive fault patches shows how a moderate-sized earthquake can be generated in an intraplate setting.

Paper (GRL, 2024)

Rupture model of the 2024 Mw 4.8 Tewksbury, New Jersey, earthquake

Millions of people around New York City felt this earthquake, but the epicentral area experienced no known significant damage. Fault slip models from regional Lg-wave relative source time functions show the rupture propagating toward the east-northeast and down-dip on an east-dipping fault, which may account for the lack of strong shaking near the epicenter.

Paper (The Seismic Record, 2024) · Media coverage
